# Closing the Drop-Off
### How AI Reshaped the Quote Experience

Role: Lead Product Designer · Team: 1 designer, 1 PM, 2 engineers · Live, measured impact

---

Every filing at Azami Global starts with a quote. It was also where 40% of them died.

Our users — paralegals managing high-stakes patent filings — aren't unfamiliar with software, but they were unfamiliar with AI making suggestions in their workflow, and their job leaves no room for a wrong guess. We designed for accountability first, automation second. Abandonment dropped to **7%.**

## The hard part: trust

A wrong AI suggestion here isn't a UX inconvenience — it's a legal filing error with real cost. So the design problem wasn't generating a suggestion. It was earning the right to be trusted with one, by someone whose job is to catch exactly this kind of mistake.

Two decisions, each made across live alternatives:

**Plain-language reasoning, not a confidence score.** A statistical score demands a mental model paralegals don't use day to day. A one-line reason — "based on similar filings in this classification" — matches how they already justify a call to a partner.

*[Image: "why" tooltip open, reasoning shown in plain language]*

**Field-level override, not an accept/reject toggle.** Correcting one suggestion shouldn't cost the other four. This mirrors how a paralegal already reviews a colleague's draft — fix what's wrong, keep what's right, stay the final authority.

*[Image: before/after — one region manually corrected, rest untouched]*
