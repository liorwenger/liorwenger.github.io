Wix Reviews Widget

Wix had no native reviews product. I led the design of their first-party solution – from a blank page to a widget used by 70,000 stores in its first rollout.

Context

Store owners on Wix were stuck using third-party review apps that looked off-brand, broke on mobile, and took hours to configure. The gap was obvious – Wix needed its own reviews product, built to feel native across millions of different stores. This project was the answer.

The Problem

Third-party apps weren't good enough

Store owners on Wix had two options: use a third-party reviews app from the App Market, or go without. Both were bad. Third-party apps were hard to integrate, offered limited customization, and never truly matched a site's design.

"We want a product reviews application that has the look and feel of Wix."

– Direct user feedback from Wix community

Users weren't just complaining about aesthetics. The friction of setting up external tools meant many store owners simply didn't collect reviews at all – leaving a huge trust signal off the table.

Key Design Decisions

Three things that were actually difficult

Building a "reviews widget" sounds simple. In practice it meant solving three real design problems: making one widget feel native across millions of different brands, connecting two users who never share a screen, and shipping without harming the metrics Wix's business depends on.

Challenge 01

How do you build one widget
for a million different brands?

Every Wix store looks different. A generic widget that doesn't match the brand is exactly what users hated about third-party apps. The widget had to feel native everywhere – without overwhelming store owners with settings.

The decision

Three independent layers: Display (what to show), Design (how it looks), Layout (how it's arranged). Each is self-contained – owners can go as deep as they want without disturbing the rest.

Challenge 02

Two users. One system.
Neither sees the other's screen.

The Site Owner sets up the widget and moderates reviews. The Buyer purchases and writes a review days later. They never interact directly – but the automated email that connects them is the only reason any review ever gets submitted.

The decision

Mapped both flows side-by-side from day one, then worked backwards to make every handoff airtight. The new Business Manager Reviews tab gave owners full visibility into Published, Pending, and Rejected reviews without hunting through settings.

Challenge 03

Where does it live –
and does it actually matter?

Two options: the App Market (visible, but a context switch away) or the Plugin panel right on the product page (contextual, zero friction). Both reach the same widget. The path there determines how many people ever try it.

The decision

Plugin panel. Store owners are already on the product page when the need for reviews is most obvious – that's the moment of intent. No context switch, no second tab, no drop-off.

Challenge 04

What does the widget look like
before anyone has left a review?

If the empty state looks broken or bare, store owners remove the widget before their customers ever see it. "No reviews yet" can read as an invitation or a warning – the design decides which.

The decision

Treat it as a call to action, not a placeholder. A prominent "Write a Review" button and copy that frames the gap as an opportunity – so store owners feel confident leaving it live, and buyers feel invited to act.
